# Faculty Research #
This project relates to a newer method of measuring blood flow, touching areas in photonics and biomedical engineering. Here we list a few BU professors and their research that relate to this project:
1. Darren Roblyer, PhD (https://www.bu.edu/eng/profile/darren-roblyer-ph-d/) with the Biomedical Optical Technologies Lab (https://www.bu.edu/botlab/). One of the most relevant papers is "Speckle contrast optical spectroscopy for cuffless blood pressure estimation based on microvascular blood flow and volume oscillations" (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40809974/), which is a more complex and higher-budget version of a SCOS setup. Our goal is to imitate this but with simplified, accessible hardware on a smaller scale.
2. Xiaojun Cheng, PhD (https://www.bu.edu/eng/profile/xiaojun-cheng-ph-d/) with the Bio Speckle Dynamics Lab (https://sites.bu.edu/cheng/). This lab focuses on speckle-based and other optical techniques to measure brain function, eg in their 2025 paper "Mapping human cerebral blood flow with high-density, multi-channel speckle contrast optical spectroscopy" (https://opg.optica.org/ol/fulltext.cfm?uri=ol-50-17-5382). This study demonstrated noise-reduction techniques to make SCOS more effective at measuring blood flow in the brain.
3. Irving Bigio, PhD (https://www.bu.edu/eng/profile/irving-bigio/) with the Biomedical Optics Laboratory (https://www.bu.edu/bmo/). Their research includes optical biopsy, real-time imaging systems during surgery, and noninvasive imaging of brain function, eg "Toward optical spectroscopy-guided lung biopsy: Demonstration of tissue-type classification," though this mirrors our project less closely as it uses Elastic Scattering Spectroscopy. It is still an interesting example that combines a fiber optical sensor with machine learning.
4. Anna Devor, PhD (https://www.bu.edu/eng/profile/anna-devor-ph-d/) with the Neurovascular Imaging Laboratory (https://sites.bu.edu/nil/). An example of a related paper is "Measurements of slow tissue dynamics with short-separation speckle contrast optical spectroscopy" (https://opg.optica.org/boe/fulltext.cfm?uri=boe-14-9-4790). This demonstrates a working SCOS setup with a single laser source and multiple camera sensors, and shows the relevant fitting and analysis procedures.
